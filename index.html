Maybe the problem was never that you need better coping skills. Maybe your life has been asking more of you than it reasonably should, and no amount of self-improvement was ever going to fix that.

If you've already been through a crash, you know how to survive a bad day. This is for after that: the months where you have a little capacity back and you start wondering why you keep ending up here in the first place.

Five layers, soil to petals: your environment, your relationships, your body, your identity, and what becomes possible once the pressure eases. Different ways of asking the same question: what conditions actually let you feel like yourself? They hold together as one system here, not five separate to-do lists.

If ordinary life has always seemed to cost you more than it costs everyone else, if you can function for a while and then completely crash, if rest helps for a minute and then you're right back here: that isn't you being dramatic or bad at adulting. You don't need a diagnosis to deserve an environment that actually works for you. And if a lot of this keeps making things click into place, it might be worth exploring what neurodivergence looks like for you specifically.

Come back to any layer whenever something in your life needs it. There's no order to follow and no chapter to finish before another one counts.

Six small things that help, right now. Choose one to open it.

When your system is depleted, don't ask it to do more.

Reduce what is draining you.
Increase what is sustaining you.

Less noise. Less input. Less explaining. Less performing. Less deciding.

More food. More fluids. More sleep. More darkness. More quiet. More support.

You don't have to become functional today.

You need to create the conditions in which your body can begin to recover.

For the next 24 to 72 hours, your job is not to fix your life.

1
Stop adding stimulation. Put the phone down. Dim the lights. Reduce noise.
2
Get fluids and food into your body. Water; regular meals; protein + carbohydrates.
3
Sleep and rest more than you normally would if your body is asking for it.
4
Cancel what can be cancelled. Work, plans, errands, unnecessary conversations.
5
Tell at least one safe person what is happening.
6
Reduce sensory input. Darker, quieter, softer, cooler/warmer according to what feels comfortable.
7
Don't make major life decisions while severely depleted.
If you are physically very unwell, suddenly deteriorating, unable to eat/drink, fainting, having chest pain, severe shortness of breath, confusion, or other concerning symptoms, seek medical care rather than assuming it's "just nervous-system overload."
